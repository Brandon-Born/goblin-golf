# Rules And Competition Direction

Goblin Golf should follow competitive disc golf rules closely while presenting them through a whimsical fantasy style.

The design target is a fun mix: serious disc golf rules and shot mechanics, playful goblin presentation.

## Rules Baseline

Use PDGA rules as the gameplay baseline. The Disc Golf Pro Tour states that all PDGA rules are in effect at DGPT-sanctioned events, so a "DGPT-style" rules approach should start from the current PDGA Official Rules of Disc Golf and Competition Manual.

Primary references:

- PDGA Official Rules and Regulations of Disc Golf: https://www.pdga.com/rules
- DGPT Rules and Regulations: https://www.dgpt.com/rules/

The rules should be treated as a design reference, not legal text copied into the game. The prototype should simplify presentation while preserving core outcomes.

## Competitive Rules To Model Early

The first playable hole should model these concepts:

- Stroke count
- Par
- Tee shot
- Lie after each throw
- Completing the hole by holing out
- Out-of-bounds penalty
- Relief from out-of-bounds
- Drop zone support if the hole needs one

## Out Of Bounds

Out-of-bounds should behave like serious disc golf, even when the boundary is whimsical.

Examples:

- A sparkling creek can be OB water.
- A cursed cart path can be OB pavement.
- Spectator ropes can define OB.
- A mushroom garden can be a marked restricted area.

Prototype behavior:

- If the disc lands OB, add one penalty stroke.
- Move the lie to the appropriate relief position or drop zone.
- Clearly explain the result with concise feedback.

The player-facing language can stay simple: "Out of bounds. One penalty stroke."

## Fantasy Hazards

Fantasy hazards should be mechanically clear and rules-compatible.

Recommended hazard categories:

- Out-of-bounds areas that add a penalty stroke.
- Relief areas that force a new lie.
- Obstacles that block or shape shot lines.
- Wind zones that affect flight without changing rules.
- Rough that reduces control or stance quality without inventing extra penalties.

Avoid hazards that feel random or unfair. Whimsy should come from presentation, not hidden rule changes.

## Serious Mechanics, Playful Presentation

The game should communicate real disc golf ideas through approachable presentation:

- Lie
- Par
- Stroke
- OB
- Drop zone
- Wind
- Disc stability
- Hyzer and anhyzer
- Putting

The UI can use plain-language explanations first, then reveal disc golf terminology as players learn.

## Prototype Rule Simplifications

The proof of concept does not need every official edge case.

Reasonable simplifications:

- No group rulings.
- No tournament timing rules.
- No stance violation enforcement beyond basic lie position.
- No lost-disc search timer.
- No provisional throws.
- No full competition manual procedures.

The important part is that normal play, OB, relief, and scoring feel consistent with real disc golf.
