# Art Direction

Goblin Golf should use high-quality pixel art that pays homage to classic 16-bit sports games without being limited by historical 16-bit constraints.

## Visual Target

The style should feel nostalgic, colorful, and readable, but the game should use modern quality where it helps:

- Higher resolution pixel art
- More animation frames than old console games typically allowed
- Richer color palettes
- Layered backgrounds
- Expressive character portraits
- Smooth camera movement
- Modern UI clarity

The goal is not strict retro accuracy. The goal is a polished pixel-art sports fantasy game that feels inspired by classic arcade and console golf games.

## Tone

The style should be lighthearted and whimsical while the underlying rules and mechanics remain serious enough for disc golf players to recognize.

Recommended tone mix:

- Serious disc golf structure
- Playful character animation
- Fantasy course hazards
- Comedic reactions
- Clear competitive feedback
- Bright, readable environments

## Character Art

The three starting goblins should have distinct silhouettes and personalities at mobile size.

Guidelines:

- Exaggerate posture and throwing style.
- Use readable color blocking.
- Keep faces expressive even in small portraits.
- Animate idle poses, throw windups, missed-putt reactions, and successful putts.
- Avoid overly detailed designs that become muddy on phone screens.

## Course Art

Courses should mix recognizable disc golf layouts with fantasy flavor.

Examples:

- Fairways through oversized mushrooms
- Stone ruins as mando-style obstacles
- Scattered bones as environmental flavor
- Rope-marked out-of-bounds paths
- Sparkling creek hazards
- Goblin gallery areas
- Wind flags, banners, and course signage

Fantasy elements should support readability. A player should immediately understand what is playable, what is out of bounds, and what affects the shot.

## UI Art

The UI should be pixel-inspired but modern in usability.

Guidelines:

- Use crisp pixel-style icons where possible.
- Keep text legible on mobile screens.
- Avoid tiny ornamental borders around important controls.
- Use clear aim, power, release-angle, disc, crosshair, and wind indicators.
- Let menus feel playful without hiding core information.

## First Prototype Asset Pass

Use readable gameplay placeholders first so the shot loop can be tuned quickly. After the scaffold and screens exist, title and character presentation can receive generated pixel-art-inspired bitmap art.
